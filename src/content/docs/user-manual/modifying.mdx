Most of the time in CAD is spent changing what is already there. This chapter covers how to choose objects, then the tools that move, copy and reshape them.

## Selecting objects

You can select first and then start a command, or start a command and select when it asks `Select objects:`. Both work the same.

| To select | Do this |
|---|---|
| One object | Click it. Each click adds to the selection. |
| Everything inside an area | Click an empty spot, then click a second corner to the **right**. Only objects completely inside the box are selected (a **window**). |
| Everything the area touches | Click an empty spot, then click a second corner to the **left**. Objects inside the box or crossing its edge are selected (a **crossing**). |
| Inside a freehand shape | Press the mouse button on an empty spot and drag around the objects. This draws a **lasso**: dragged to the right it selects like a window, to the left like a crossing. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> while dragging to switch between window, crossing and fence. |
| Everything | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> with no command running. |
| Less | <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click a selected object to remove it. |
| Nothing | <kbd>Esc</kbd>. |

As the cursor passes over an object, it is highlighted, so you can see what a click will pick. If you prefer to drag boxes instead of clicking two corners, set [PICKDRAG](/reference/system-variables/pickdrag/) to `1`. In version 0.8.0 it goes back to `2` the next time Musa CAD starts.

When a command asks `Select objects:`, keep selecting, then press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or right-click to finish. You can also type a selection keyword there, such as `ALL`, `L` for the last object drawn, `P` for the previous selection, or `F` for a fence. [SELECT](/reference/commands/select/) lists them all.

## Grips

Selected objects show small squares called **grips** at their key points: the ends and middle of a line, the centre and quadrants of a circle, each vertex of a polyline.

Click a grip and move the cursor to stretch the object from that point. Click again, or type a point, to place it. While a grip is active, press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> to step through the other grip modes: **STRETCH**, **MOVE**, **ROTATE**, **SCALE** and **MIRROR**. Type `C` to make copies instead of moving the original, `B` to choose another base point, and `X` to stop.

Right-click a polyline's grip for a menu of what that grip can do, such as adding or removing a vertex.

## Change shapes

<CommandList label="Commands that change shapes" names={['TRIM', 'EXTEND', 'OFFSET', 'FILLET', 'CHAMFER', 'BREAK', 'BREAKATPOINT', 'JOIN', 'LENGTHEN', 'EXPLODE', 'PEDIT', 'SPLINEDIT', 'REVERSE', 'OVERKILL']} />

TRIM and EXTEND are the two you will use most. Both start in **Quick** mode, where every object is an edge and you just click the part to cut off or lengthen. Hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> to switch between them while you work.

## Change properties

Every object has a layer, a colour, a linetype, a lineweight and more. Usually these come from its layer ("ByLayer").

- The **Properties** palette (`PR`) shows the properties of the selection and lets you change any of them.
- **Match Properties** (`MA`) copies properties from one object to others.
- To move objects to another layer, select them and change **Layer** in the Properties palette, or use [LAYMCH](/reference/commands/laymch/) to match the layer of an object you pick.

<CommandList label="Property commands" names={['PROPERTIES', 'MATCHPROP', 'CHPROP', 'CHANGE', 'COPYTOLAYER', 'DRAWORDER']} />

## Undo and redo

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> or `U` undoes the last command. Redo puts it back. Inside many commands, such as LINE and TRIM, the **Undo** option takes back just the last step without leaving the command.
