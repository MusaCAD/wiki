You draw at full size, then put the drawing on paper. Musa CAD keeps the two apart, the way most CAD programs do: the drawing lives in **model space**, and each sheet is a **layout**.

## Model space and layouts

**Model space** is where you draw, at real size: a 4 metre wall is 4000 units long in a millimetre drawing. You can plot straight from model space, which is fine for simple drawings.

A **layout** is a sheet of paper, with a border, a title block and notes drawn at paper size. Layouts are also called paper space. The tabs below the drawing switch between model space and each layout. Right-click a layout tab to add, rename or delete a layout.

## Viewports

A **viewport** is a window on a layout that shows model space at a scale you choose, such as 1:50. A layout can have several viewports, each showing a different part of the model at its own scale.

1. On a layout, type `MV` for MVIEW and click two corners. The viewport shows the whole model.
2. Double-click inside the viewport to work in model space through it. Zoom and pan until the right part shows, or set its scale with MVIEW's **Scale** option.
3. Type `PS` for PSPACE to go back to the sheet. The viewport keeps the view you left it with.

Each viewport can hide layers of its own with VPLAYER, or with the **VP Freeze** column of the Layer Properties Manager while you work inside it. Use it to show dimensions in one viewport but not another.

In model space itself, VPORTS splits the window into tiles that each show the drawing from their own view. That is for working, not for plotting.

## Plotting

Type `PLOT`, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>P</kbd>. The Plot window sets:

| Setting | Choices |
|---|---|
| **Printer/plotter** | PDF, or a printer on your computer. |
| **Paper size** | ISO A4, A3, A2, A1 and A0, ANSI A (Letter) and ANSI B (Tabloid). |
| **Plot area** | **Display** (what is on screen), **Extents** (everything), or **Window** (a rectangle you pick). A layout plots its sheet. |
| **Scale** | **Fit to paper**, or an exact scale such as 1 mm to 5 units. **Center the plot**, or an **Offset**. |
| **Plot style (CTB)** | In colour, **Monochrome (all black)**, or **Grayscale**. |
| **Plot object lineweights** | Lines plot at their lineweights. Untick it for thin lines everywhere. |
| **Orientation** | **Portrait** or **Landscape**. |

Click **Preview…** to see the page before you plot. PDFs are real vector files, so lines stay sharp at any zoom.

A **page setup** saves these settings under a name, so every plot of a layout comes out the same. PAGESETUP manages them.

If a plot comes out wrong, see [A plot or PDF looks wrong](/troubleshooting/plot-looks-wrong/).

## Many sheets at once

PUBLISH plots the model and any layouts you choose into one PDF, one page each. To plot a whole folder of drawings, use the command line instead:

```sh
for f in *.musa; do musacad --plot "$f" "${f%.musa}.pdf" --paper A3; done
```

[The command line](/scripting/command-line/) lists every plot option, such as `--scale 1:50`, `--window` and `--monochrome`.

## Dashes in viewports

With `PSLTSCALE` at 1, the default, dashed lines seen through a viewport are scaled by the viewport's scale. Dashes then look the same length on paper in every viewport, whatever its scale.
