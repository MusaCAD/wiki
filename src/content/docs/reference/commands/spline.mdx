SPLINE draws a smooth, free-form curve. Use it for shapes that are not made of lines and arcs, such as a contour line or a curved edge.

## How it works

Type `SPL` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Musa CAD shows the current settings, then asks for points. Click the points the curve should pass through. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to finish.

```musacad
Command: SPL
Current settings: Method=Fit   Knots=Chord
Specify first point or [Method/Knots/Object]: 0,0
Enter next point or [start Tangency/toLerance]: 20,25
Enter next point or [end Tangency/toLerance/Undo]: 40,5
Enter next point or [end Tangency/toLerance/Undo/Close]: 60,30
Enter next point or [end Tangency/toLerance/Undo/Close]: 80,10
Enter next point or [end Tangency/toLerance/Undo/Close]: ↵
```

## Fit and CV

SPLINE has two methods. Switch between them with **Method**. Musa CAD keeps your choice for next time.

- **Fit** (the default): the curve passes through every point you give.
- **CV**: the points are control vertices. They make a frame that pulls the curve toward it. The curve touches only the first and the last point.

With CV, the prompts change. SPLINE starts with `Current settings: Method=CV   Degree=3` and `Specify first point or [Method/Degree/Object]:`, then asks `Enter next point:`, `Enter next point or [Undo]:` and `Enter next point or [Close/Undo]:`.

## Options

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Method** (`M`) | `Enter spline creation method [Fit/CV] <Fit>:`. Choose Fit or CV. |
| **Knots** (`K`) | Fit only. `Enter knot parameterization [Chord/Square root/Uniform] <Chord>:`. Sets how the curve is spread between your points. The three choices give slightly different shapes when the points are unevenly spaced. |
| **Degree** (`D`) | CV only. `Enter degree of spline <3>:`, a whole number from 1 to 10. Degree 1 joins the points with straight lines; the default, 3, gives a smooth curve. |
| **Object** (`O`) | Asks `Select spline-fit polylines:`. Each polyline you select becomes a spline. A polyline smoothed with the Spline option of [PEDIT](/reference/commands/pedit/) keeps exactly the same curve. Any other polyline becomes a spline through its vertices. |
| **start Tangency** (`T`) | Fit, before the second point. `Specify start tangent:`. Click a point, and the curve leaves the first point heading toward it. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to leave the start free. |
| **end Tangency** (`T`) | Fit, from the second point on. `Specify end tangent:`. Click a point, and the curve ends heading toward it. This also finishes the spline. |
| **toLerance** (`L`) | Fit only. `Specify fit tolerance <0.0000>:`. With 0 the curve passes through every point. A larger value lets it pass within that distance of the points, which can make it smoother. |
| **Undo** (`U`) | Removes the last point. |
| **Close** (`C`) | Joins the curve back to the first point and finishes. With Fit, the curve runs smoothly through the join. You need at least three points. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Finishes the spline. |

## Good to know

- <kbd>Esc</kbd> throws away the spline you are drawing. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to keep it.
- A spline needs at least two points. Pressing <kbd>Enter</kbd> after one says `A spline needs at least two points.` and nothing is drawn.
- **Close** with fewer than three points says `Close needs at least three points.`
- The method, knots and degree are kept until you close Musa CAD. The tolerance starts at 0 every time.
- A tolerance has no effect on a closed spline, or on one with a start or end tangent.
- With fewer points than the degree plus one, the degree is lowered to fit. Two points give a straight line.
- A tangent point on top of the end point is refused with `The tangent needs a point away from the spline's end.`
- A Fit spline keeps its fit points. Its grips sit on them, and dragging one fits the curve again.

## Compared with AutoCAD

SPLINE uses AutoCAD's prompts for its Fit and CV methods, with the same options: Knots, Degree, Object, start and end Tangency, toLerance, Undo and Close.
