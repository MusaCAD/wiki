ARC draws an arc, which is part of a circle. The quickest way is to give three points on it: where it starts, a point it passes through, and where it ends.

## How it works

Type `A` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Give the start point, a second point on the arc, and the end point. Musa CAD draws the arc through all three and the command ends.

```musacad
Command: A
Specify start point of arc or [Center]: 0,0
Specify second point of arc or [Center/End]: 30,20
Specify end point of arc: 60,0
```

If the three points lie in a straight line, no arc passes through them. Musa CAD says `*Invalid*` and asks for the end point again.

## Options

Besides three points, you can build an arc from its start, its centre and its end, and finish it with an angle, a chord length, a direction or a radius. The **Arc** list on the ribbon starts each of these ways for you, such as **Start, Center, End** or **Start, End, Radius**.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Center** (`C`) | At the first prompt, give the centre first: `Specify center point of arc:`, then `Specify start point of arc:`. At the second prompt, give the centre after the start point. Either way, Musa CAD then asks `Specify end point of arc (hold Ctrl to switch direction) or [Angle/chord Length]:`. |
| **End** (`E`) | At the second prompt, give the end point next. Musa CAD then asks `Specify center point of arc (hold Ctrl to switch direction) or [Angle/Direction/Radius]:`. |
| **Angle** (`A`) | After the centre or the end: the included angle, which is how far the arc sweeps, in degrees. A positive angle goes counter-clockwise, a negative one clockwise. |
| **chord Length** (`L`) | After the centre: the straight distance from the start to the end. A positive length draws the shorter arc, a negative one the longer arc. |
| **Direction** (`D`) | After the end: the direction the arc leaves the start point in. Type an angle, or click a point to aim it. |
| **Radius** (`R`) | After the end: the radius. A positive radius draws the shorter arc, a negative one the longer arc. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> at **Specify start point of arc** | Continues from the end of the last line or arc you drew, carrying on smoothly in its direction. Musa CAD asks only `Specify end point of arc (hold Ctrl to switch direction):`. |

## Start, centre and end

With a centre, the start point sets the radius. The end point you give only says where the arc stops: the arc ends where the line from the centre through that point crosses it.

```musacad
Command: A
Specify start point of arc or [Center]: 130,0
Specify second point of arc or [Center/End]: C
Specify center point of arc: 110,0
Specify end point of arc (hold Ctrl to switch direction) or [Angle/chord Length]: @28<120
```

Here `@28<120` is a point 28 from the centre at 120 degrees, so the arc sweeps 120 degrees.

## Which way the arc goes

Arcs run counter-clockwise from the start point, unless you say otherwise. Where a prompt says `(hold Ctrl to switch direction)`, hold <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> as you click to draw the arc clockwise instead. While you move the cursor, the rubber band shows the arc the click will make.

A three-point arc always goes from the first point, through the second, to the third, whichever way round that is.

## Good to know

- `*Invalid*` means no arc fits what you gave: three points in a line, a chord longer than the circle is wide, or a radius shorter than half the distance from start to end. Musa CAD stays at the same prompt, so try another value.
- **Direction** and Continue already fix which way the arc leaves, so holding <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> changes nothing there, even though the prompt mentions it.
- Pressing <kbd>Enter</kbd> at the first prompt when you have not drawn a line or arc yet does nothing. The prompt stays.
- Each arc is a separate object. To draw lines and arcs as one object, use the arc mode of [PLINE](/reference/commands/pline/).
- <kbd>Esc</kbd> cancels the command. Nothing is drawn.

## Compared with AutoCAD

ARC offers AutoCAD's methods: three points, the Center and End branches, the centre first, and Continue with <kbd>Enter</kbd>. The **Arc** list on the ribbon starts each of its eleven ways.
