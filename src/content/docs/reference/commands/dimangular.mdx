DIMANGULAR dimensions an angle. Pick two lines and it measures the angle between them, with an arc and an arrow at each end. It can also measure how far an arc turns, an angle on a circle, or the angle between three points you click.

## How it works

Type `DAN` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Pick the first line, then the second. Move the cursor: an arc follows it between the lines, with the angle written on it. Click to place the arc. The dimension is drawn and the command ends.

Two lines that cross make four angles. You get the one your click stands in, and the arc passes through the point you click. Click between the lines for the narrow angle, or on the other side of one line for the wide angle next to it.

```musacad
Command: DAN
Select arc, circle, line, or <specify vertex>: (pick the first line)
Select second line: (pick the second line)
Specify dimension arc line location or [Mtext/Text/Angle/Quadrant]: (click between the two lines)
Angular dimension placed.
Dimension created from object.
```

### Other things you can pick

What is under the cursor at the first pick decides the next prompt:

- **An arc:** Musa CAD measures the angle the arc spans, seen from its centre. It goes straight to the arc location prompt.
- **A circle:** the point where you picked the circle is the first end. Musa CAD asks `Specify second angle endpoint:`, and measures the angle between the two points, seen from the centre.
- **Three points:** press <kbd>Enter</kbd> at the first prompt. Click the vertex (the corner of the angle), then a point on each side.

```musacad
Command: DAN
Select arc, circle, line, or <specify vertex>: ↵
Specify angle vertex: 0,0
Specify first angle endpoint: 50,0
Specify second angle endpoint: 0,30
Specify dimension arc line location or [Mtext/Text/Angle/Quadrant]: (click inside the corner)
Angular dimension placed.
```

With three points, or with a circle, clicking outside the angle measures the rest of the turn instead. That is how you dimension an angle bigger than 180°.

## Options

These options are offered while you place the arc. After you choose one, Musa CAD asks for the arc location again.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mtext** (`M`) or **Text** (`T`) | Type your own text at `Enter dimension text (<> for the measurement):`. `<>` stands for the measured angle. Text without `<>` replaces it. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> without typing to use the measurement. |
| **Angle** (`A`) | Turns the text. At `Specify angle of dimension text:` type an angle in degrees. `0` or <kbd>Enter</kbd> stands it upright again. |
| **Quadrant** (`Q`) | Choose the angle with a point of its own. At `Specify quadrant:` click inside the angle you want measured. After that, the arc can go anywhere you click, even outside that angle. |

## Good to know

- A "line" can be a line or one straight segment of a polyline.
- If you type a point at the first prompt with nothing under the cursor, Musa CAD takes it as a pick on a line.
- Two parallel lines have no angle between them. Musa CAD then says `Could not dimension that object -- select a line, circle, or arc.` when you place the arc, and draws nothing.
- The value follows the current dimension style. The Standard style writes decimal degrees with two places, such as `40.00°`. Change the angle format in [DIMSTYLE](/reference/commands/dimstyle/), on the **Primary Units** tab.
- The arc has a grip, so you can drag it nearer or further later. [ROTATE](/reference/commands/rotate/), [MIRROR](/reference/commands/mirror/) and [SCALE](/reference/commands/scale/) keep it.
- When you save as DXF, the arc's distance from the vertex is not written. Read back, the dimension shows the smaller angle.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd> cancels without drawing anything. [U](/reference/commands/u/) removes the dimension in one step.

## Compared with AutoCAD

As in AutoCAD, the place you click decides which angle is measured, and **Quadrant** lets you choose it separately.
